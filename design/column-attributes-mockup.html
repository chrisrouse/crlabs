Column attributes — three treatments

All three show the same column: Amount, a currency field. The blue-edged blocks are the parts that appear because of the type — on a text column they would not render at all. That type-awareness is the main idea being tested; the three treatments differ in how you get to the settings, not in what the settings are.

A — Sectioned panel
One column at a time, settings grouped by purpose. Closest to today's Advanced block, just organised and type-aware. Cheapest to build. Weak point: no overview — you cannot see all columns or reorder them from here.

C — Grid plus drawer
Keeps today's scannable table for the things that are genuinely tabular — order, label, width, the flags — and opens a type-aware drawer for the rest. Keeps the overview AND gets type-awareness. Weak point: two places to edit, so it needs a clear rule about what lives where.

Open questions these are meant to settle. Whether the scannable grid is worth keeping (only C keeps it). Whether min/max decimals should replace today's single Scale — Avonni splits them and the datatable's own type attributes are minimumFractionDigits/maximumFractionDigits, so Scale is our invention sitting on top. And whether conditional formatting belongs inside the column panel, as drawn here, or as its own tab across all columns.
